What this tool tells you

Just enter your cat’s or dog’s age to instantly see roughly how old they would be in human years (for cats and small-to-medium dogs). It also shows when the senior stage typically begins. Useful when thinking about checkups or when to switch to senior food.

How to use

  1. Choose cat or dog (small or medium).
  2. Enter your pet’s age (0.5 for 6 months old).
  3. The moment you type, the approximate human age and life stage appear automatically.

How it is calculated

  • 1 pet year = about 15 human years / 2 years = about 24 / then about +4 human years per year
  • The idea is that pets grow to the equivalent of a 24-year-old human within their first two years, then age more slowly — a guideline formula introduced by Western veterinary organizations.
  • Example: a 5-year-old cat → 24 + 4×3 = about 36 human years
  • The old rule of thumb “human age = pet age × 7” does not fit the fast growth of the early years, so this formula is now the mainstream guideline.

Sources and assumptions

  • The formula is a guideline widely used by veterinary organizations in the West (for example, the life-stage guidelines of the American Animal Hospital Association (AAHA), in English). It is a rough guide, not a scientifically exact conversion.

Assumptions and notes

  • Large dogs (roughly 25 kg and over) age faster, and this formula does not apply to them (large dogs are considered senior at around 7–8 years).
  • Actual aging varies a lot with breed, environment and the individual animal. For health care, consult your veterinarian.

Frequently asked questions

Is “1 dog year = 7 human years” wrong?

It is an oversimplification. Dogs and cats race past the equivalent of a 20-year-old human in their first one to two years, so ×7 does not fit early life. The formula used here — fast at first, slower afterwards — is considered a closer guideline to reality.

From what age is a pet senior?

Cats and small-to-medium dogs enter their mid-40s in human terms at around age 7. From age 7 it is often recommended to move from one checkup a year to two.

How long do cats live?

The average lifespan of indoor-only cats is reported to be around 16 years. Cats living past 20 (about 96 human years) are no longer rare.
