Tablespoons and teaspoons → grams

The same tablespoon weighs a different number of grams depending on the ingredient (water 15 g, sugar 9 g, soy sauce 18 g and so on), so this calculates the weight from the spoons and the ingredient.

Grams → tablespoons and teaspoons

The reverse conversion for measuring a recipe written in grams — like “30 g of sugar” — with spoons instead of a scale.

What this tool tells you

“How many ml is 3 tablespoons?” “How many tablespoons is 50 ml?” — just type a number to restate the tablespoon, teaspoon, cup and ml amounts in Japanese recipes. These are the Japanese kitchen-measure sizes (tablespoon 15 ml, teaspoon 5 ml, cup 200 ml — note that a Japanese cup differs from a US cup). Useful when you have no measuring spoons or cup at hand, or want to scale a recipe.

How to use

  1. Enter the amount (the number) from the recipe.
  2. Choose its unit (tablespoon, teaspoon, cup or ml).
  3. The amount in every unit appears the moment you type — no button needed.

How it is calculated

  • 1 teaspoon = 5 ml / 1 tablespoon = 15 ml / 1 cup = 200 ml (the standard sizes of Japanese measuring spoons and cups)
  • 1 tablespoon = 3 teaspoons, and 1 cup = 13 tablespoons + 1 teaspoon.
  • ml and cc are the same amount (1 ml = 1 cc).

Sources (primary information)

  • The conversion is based on the capacities of standard Japanese measuring utensils (teaspoon 5 ml, tablespoon 15 ml, cup 200 ml).

Assumptions and notes

  • This is a volume conversion. Weight (g) depends on the density of the ingredient (1 tablespoon is 15 g of water, but about 12 g of oil, about 9 g of Japanese white sugar and about 18 g of salt).
  • One cup in overseas recipes is different (about 240 ml in the US, 250 ml in Australia). The rice cup that comes with Japanese rice cookers is 180 ml — also a different thing.

Frequently asked questions

How many grams is 1 tablespoon?

It depends on the ingredient. As a guide: 15 g for water, vinegar or sake; about 18 g for soy sauce or mirin; about 12 g for oil or butter; about 9 g for Japanese white sugar; about 18 g for salt. This tool converts by volume (ml).

What can I use instead of a measuring spoon?

A plastic-bottle cap holds about 7.5 ml (half a tablespoon), and a curry spoon holds a little under 1 tablespoon as a guide. For baking, where accuracy matters, proper measuring utensils are recommended.

How much is a “pinch” or a “dash” in Japanese recipes?

As a guide, a dash (shōshō) is the amount you pick up between thumb and index finger (about 1/8 teaspoon), and a pinch (hitotsumami) is the amount picked up with three fingers (about 1/5–1/4 teaspoon).
