What this tool tells you

Type one area figure and instantly see what it comes to in tsubo, square meters (㎡) and tatami mats (jo). Japanese property listings and floor plans mix all three units — a room advertised as “6畳” (6 jo) is written in ㎡ on the contract — so this is handy when you are flat-hunting in Japan and want to picture the real size.

How to use

  1. Enter the area you want to convert (for example 100).
  2. Choose the unit of that value (㎡, tsubo or jo).
  3. All three units appear the moment you type — no button needed.

How it is calculated

  • 1 tsubo = 400/121 ㎡ ≈ 3.31 ㎡ (1 ㎡ ≈ 0.3025 tsubo)
    From the definition 1 shaku = 10/33 m, one tsubo is (6 shaku)² = 400/121 ㎡. Note that the traditional shakkanho units (tsubo) are not permitted for transactions or certification under Japan's Measurement Act, so a figure in tsubo is a customary reference value.
  • 1 tatami mat (jo) = 1.62 ㎡
    This is the standard that applies when a Japanese property advertisement states “◯ jo” (at least 1.62 ㎡ per mat, measured on a wall-center basis).

Assumptions and notes

  • Real tatami mats differ by region and type (Kyoma about 1.82 ㎡, Chukyoma about 1.66 ㎡, Edoma about 1.55 ㎡, Danchima about 1.45 ㎡). This tool uses the single advertising standard of 1.62 ㎡.
  • For the official area of a property, check the registry record and the drawings, which are stated in ㎡.

Frequently asked questions

How many tatami mats is one tsubo?

At the advertising standard of 1.62 ㎡ per mat, 1 tsubo is about 2.04 jo. The customary rule of thumb is “1 tsubo ≈ 2 tatami mats”.

Why does the same property show different areas on different documents?

Advertisements usually use the “wall-center” area measured to the middle of the walls, while the property registry uses the “inner-edge” area measured inside the walls, so the same room can show different numbers.

A “6 jo” room felt smaller than I expected. Why?

Real tatami sizes vary by region and building (Edoma and Danchima mats are smaller than Kyoma), and a listing may say “6 jo” as long as it meets the standard of 1.62 ㎡ × 6 = 9.72 ㎡ or more.
