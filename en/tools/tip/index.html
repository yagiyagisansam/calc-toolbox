What tip rate did I actually pay? (reverse calculation)

Works out the tip rate you really paid, from the total on your card statement or receipt.

There is a service charge — do I still need to tip?

If the receipt already includes “Service Charge” or “Gratuity”, this tells you whether to add anything and roughly how much.

What this tool tells you

Enter the amount, pick a rate, and instantly see how much to tip at a restaurant abroad, along with the total including the tip and the amount per person. Handy if you live in Japan, where tipping is not customary and there is nothing to practise on, and you are heading to the United States or Hawaii.

How to use

  1. Enter the amount on the receipt (in the United States it is usual to calculate on the pre-tax food and drink total).
  2. Pick the tip rate (18% if you are unsure).
  3. If you are splitting the bill, enter the number of people and the amount per person appears automatically.

How it is calculated

  • Tip = amount × tip rate ÷ 100 / Total = amount + tip / Per person = total ÷ number of people
  • Example: 18% of $85.50 → a tip of $15.39 and a total of $100.89
  • At restaurants in the United States the usual range is roughly 15–20% (in recent years 18–20% has become the norm). If a service charge or gratuity is already included, no extra tip is needed.

Sources (primary information)

  • This is plain arithmetic. Tipping rates and customs differ by country, city and type of business — check the guidance of the local tourist board or a guidebook.

Assumptions and notes

  • If the receipt says “Gratuity Included” or “Service Charge”, the tip is already in there. Be careful not to pay twice.
  • Tipping culture varies enormously between countries. In Japan, China and South Korea it is basically not expected; in Europe around 5–10% or rounding up the bill is common.
  • If you pay by card, write the amount in the Tip line of the receipt and fill in the Total yourself.

Frequently asked questions

Do I calculate the tip on the amount before or after tax?

In the United States the original custom is to calculate on the pre-tax subtotal. Calculating on the tax-inclusive total makes only a small difference, and there is no problem with paying a little more.

Do I need to tip at fast food places and cafés?

At places where you order at the counter, generally no — even if a tablet shows you a tip screen, you can choose “No Tip”. At restaurants where you are served at your table, yes.

What is usual at hotels and in taxis?

As a rough guide, $1–2 per bag for a hotel bellhop, $2–5 per night for housekeeping, and around 15% of the fare for a taxi.
